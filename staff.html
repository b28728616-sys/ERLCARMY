Staff Moderation Handbook

Welcome to the Army Roleplay Moderation Team!

We are excited to have you as a part of our staff team. Becoming a staff member means you are trusted with the responsibility of helping maintain a professional, fair, and enjoyable environment for everyone in our community.

This handbook was created to provide staff members with the information and resources needed to properly moderate the server, handle situations professionally, and understand the expectations that come with being part of the ARP staff team.

Inside this handbook, you will find information regarding:

  • Moderation commands
  • Punishment procedures
  • Staff expectations
  • Roleplay standards
  • Presidential roleplay operations
  • Convoy procedures
  • Staff professionalism

All staff members are expected to read and understand this handbook before actively moderating.

Staff Expectations

As a member of the ARP Staff Team, you represent the community whenever you are on duty. Your actions, communication, and decisions reflect the reputation of the entire staff team.

Staff members are expected to:

  • Remain professional at all times
  • Treat players fairly and respectfully
  • Use moderation powers responsibly
  • Follow the punishment guidelines
  • Avoid abusing commands or permissions
  • Communicate properly with players and other staff members

Staff powers are a responsibility, not a privilege to be used for personal reasons.

Command Usage Guidelines

When using moderation commands, always ensure you are selecting the correct player before executing the command.

Examples of moderation commands include:

  • :bring
  • :kill
  • :kick
  • Other staff commands

Most commands can be executed by entering approximately the first three letters of a player's username.

If you accidentally use a command on the wrong player, you are expected to correct the situation immediately.

Never use commands unnecessarily or for personal benefit.

Punishment Guidelines

The punishments listed below are the recommended actions staff members should take when handling rule violations.

Staff members should not create their own punishments or change punishments without proper approval.

When determining punishment, consider:

  • The severity of the offense
  • The impact on roleplay
  • Whether the player has repeated the offense

Repeated offenses may result in increased punishment, including moving from a warning to a kick or ban.

Punishment Documentation

Before issuing any punishment, staff members must inform the player of what rule they violated and what punishment they are receiving.

Use:

:pm (username) (reason) [punishment and duration]

Example:

:pm Player123 RDM [Warning + Jail Time]

Players should always understand why action was taken against them.

If a punishment is issued without proper explanation, it may be considered invalid unless it involves an extreme offense.

Staff Duty Requirements

Staff members may begin moderating when the server has at least:

5 active players

To begin your staff shift:

  1. Join the ARP game server.
  2. Join the ARP Office (Sheriff Team).
  3. Equip your staff uniform through the wardrobe.
  4. Wear the uniform assigned to your rank.
  5. Log into Melonly using your Discord account connected to ARP.
  6. Navigate to:
    Dashboard → Army Roleplay → Moderator Panel
  7. Select:
    Start Shift

Shift Abuse Policy

Staff members are expected to actively moderate while on shift.

Shift grinding is prohibited.

Examples include:

  • Leaving your shift active after leaving the game
  • Remaining AFK while on duty
  • Abusing the break feature
  • Keeping shifts active without actually moderating

Remember: being on duty means you are representing ARP.

Maintain professionalism, proper grammar, and respectful communication.

Presidential Roleplay Guidelines

Staff members with proper authority may host Presidential Roleplay events.

These events should be organized, realistic, and enjoyable for everyone involved.

Presidential Roles
President

The player who receives the highest number of votes.

Vice President

The player who receives the second highest number of votes.

Secret Service (SS)

Responsible for protecting the President.

Counter Assault Team (CAT)

Specialized security personnel responsible for responding to threats.

Civilians

Players participating as general citizens.

Mafia

Organized civilian groups attempting to target the President.

Election Procedures

Presidential elections should be hosted in the:

Police Department Meeting Room

Staff should prepare the area before beginning.

Recommended setup:

  • Three voting areas
  • Police barricades or crowd fences
  • Peace Timer: 500 seconds

Only use objects that improve the roleplay experience.

Election Announcements

Use:

:h Presidential elections are being hosted in the police department meeting room!

Repeat announcements approximately every minute until enough players arrive.

The :m command should only be used when necessary.

Candidate Process

Announce:

:h Thank you all for attending this election. I will now be selecting three candidates.

Select three candidates and place them into voting areas.

Candidates may give speeches before voting begins.

Voting announcement:

:h Voting has started! Please stand in the box of the candidate you are voting for.

The candidate with the most votes becomes President.

The candidate with the second most votes becomes Vice President.

Staff should record usernames to prevent confusion.

Presidential Convoy Procedures

Presidential convoys must remain organized and realistic.

Requirements:

  • Convoy stays in one organized line
  • Staff vehicle leads the convoy
  • Civilians cannot interfere with the convoy
  • No standard Liberty County preset liveries
  • Minimum of one limousine or SUV replacement

During movement:

  • No unnecessary overtaking
  • No honking
  • No ramming
  • No unnecessary sirens

Roads around the convoy should be secured.

Convoy Speed

Maintain:

15-30 MPH

or

15-30 KM/H

A Peace Timer is recommended during convoy movement.

Presidential Protection

The President may travel anywhere that provides:

  • A realistic environment
  • Fair opportunities for roleplay
  • Proper security coverage

The first destination should always be:

The White House (Hospital / ICU)

Staff should regularly announce:

  • President location
  • Vice President location
  • Remaining lives

Presidential Death Procedures

When the President is eliminated:

  1. Set a reasonable Peace Timer.
  2. Restore the President if applicable.
  3. Consider remaining lives.

The maximum Peace Timer should be:

3 minutes

Once the President loses all lives:

  • The Vice President takes control.
  • Staff may restart the scenario.
  • Staff may vote on the next roleplay event.

Communication between staff members is required.

Convoy Formation Guidelines

Recommended formation:

🔵 Local Departments
🟢 Supporting Units
🟠 Specialized Teams
🟡 Security Teams
🔴 Presidential Protection

Local departments should handle:

  • Road closures
  • Traffic control
  • Perimeter security

Specialized teams should handle:

  • Direct protection
  • Counter-assault operations
  • High-risk situations

The President and Vice President should remain positioned safely in the center of the convoy.

Army Roleplay Management

Chapter I

ERLC Staff Manual Overview

This manual establishes the authority, discipline, and expectations for ERLC command staff and moderation personnel. The organization must remain structured, realistic, and loyal to the chain of command at every level.

Annotation: Command authority is not personal power. It is a trust granted to maintain order, fairness, and military realism.

Chapter III

Moderation

Moderators are the enforcement backbone of the server. They maintain fairness, stability, and compliance with operational standards while preserving professionalism in all interactions.

  • Review incidents and enforce proper staff procedure
  • Apply consistent moderation standards across the server
  • Maintain logs, evidence, and disciplinary continuity
  • Coordinate with command staff for serious or escalated cases
Annotation: moderation must be factual, documented, and free from favoritism or emotional escalation.

Chapter IV

Command Staff

Command staff manages unit leadership, departmental discipline, and operational execution. They translate executive policy into practical command actions and maintain realistic military structure across all departments.

  • Chief of Staff coordinates executive continuity and scheduler oversight
  • Executive Council advises on policy and strategic command review
  • Department Commanders oversee department discipline and operations
  • Senior Moderators support enforcement and command review
Annotation: command staff exists to maintain doctrine, accountability, and real operational coordination across the entire ERLC structure.

Chapter VI

Special Forces Tier Structure

Special Forces units operate under the same chain of command and accountability standards as every other ERLC unit. Tier designations describe mission capability and selection standards, not permission to ignore policy or act outside command authorization.

Tier 3

ODA (Green Berets)

ODA teams specialize in unconventional warfare, partner-force support, training, advising, and complex missions requiring judgment and discretion.

  • Build and support partner capability
  • Plan missions through the approved command chain
  • Apply disciplined judgment when circumstances change

ODA Rank Structure

  • Officer leadership: Captain (CPT) ODA commander; First Lieutenant (1LT) assistant ODA commander
  • Warrant expertise: Chief Warrant Officer 3-2 (CW3-CW2) technical and operational specialists
  • Senior enlisted: Sergeant First Class (SFC) team sergeant; Master Sergeant (MSG) senior advisor
  • Team personnel: Staff Sergeant (SSG), Sergeant (SGT), Specialist (SPC), and junior enlisted support

Tier 2

Rangers

Rangers provide highly trained light-infantry capability for direct action, raids, reconnaissance, security, and rapid-response missions.

  • Execute approved objectives with speed and control
  • Maintain positive identification and target discipline
  • Report mission changes and escalation immediately

Ranger Rank Structure

  • Battalion leadership: Lieutenant Colonel (LTC) battalion commander; Command Sergeant Major (CSM) senior enlisted advisor
  • Company leadership: Captain (CPT) company commander; First Sergeant (1SG) company senior enlisted leader
  • Platoon leadership: First Lieutenant (1LT) or Second Lieutenant (2LT) platoon leader; Sergeant First Class (SFC) platoon sergeant
  • Squad leadership: Staff Sergeant (SSG), Sergeant (SGT), Specialist (SPC), and junior enlisted personnel

Tier 1

1st Special Forces Operational Detachment–Delta

Delta Force handles the most sensitive and demanding missions, including high-risk counterterrorism, hostage rescue, special reconnaissance, and precision action.

  • Operate only with specific authorization and mission limits
  • Use the minimum force necessary to accomplish the objective
  • Provide detailed post-operation justification and reporting

Delta Rank Structure

  • Unit leadership: Colonel (COL) group commander; Lieutenant Colonel (LTC) squadron commander
  • Operational leadership: Major (MAJ) or Captain (CPT) troop commander; Command Sergeant Major (CSM) senior enlisted advisor
  • Technical leadership: Chief Warrant Officer 5-1 (CW5-CW1) specialists assigned to aviation, communications, intelligence, or logistics
  • Operator leadership: Sergeant Major (SGM), First Sergeant (1SG), Master Sergeant (MSG), and Sergeant First Class (SFC)
  • Operators and support: Staff Sergeant (SSG), Sergeant (SGT), Specialist (SPC), Private First Class (PFC), and Private (PV2/PVT)
Annotation: a higher tier means greater responsibility, scrutiny, and planning requirements. It never creates a separate license to use force or bypass the rules of engagement.

Chapter VII

Special Forces Rules of Engagement

Special Forces are expected to follow the same rules of engagement as every other soldier operating in a hostile area. Unconventional missions can create difficult ethical and tactical decisions, but complexity does not remove the requirements for lawful orders, positive identification, necessity, proportionality, and command accountability.

  • Use force only when authorized, necessary, and proportionate to the threat.
  • Distinguish confirmed hostile action from suspicion, rumor, or an unverified assumption.
  • When a direct or credible imminent threat is present, act to protect life and stop the threat within mission limits.
  • When time permits, pause, communicate, de-escalate, and seek command guidance before escalating.
  • Document the facts, decision process, warnings, evidence, and proportionality of every serious use-of-force decision.
  • Expect independent review when an action is disputed; good intent does not replace a defensible justification.
  • Any operator who deliberately uses the mission as cover for unnecessary or excessive force is subject to removal, discipline, and further investigation.
Case-study standard: historical incidents demonstrate why Special Forces decisions require review. A decision may be found lawful and still receive investigation when the facts are uncertain. Operators must be prepared to explain what they knew, what threat they perceived, what alternatives existed, and why their response was proportionate.
Command standard: no tier may conduct extrajudicial action, retaliatory force, or unauthorized targeting. Mission success never excuses bypassing the chain of command or concealing an incident.

ERLC Staff Manual Final Directive

ERLC staff must uphold military discipline, executive leadership, and procedural fairness. All command actions must remain within the approved chain of command, while presidential authority remains the final authority in executive matters. Moderators are responsible for enforcing standards without favoritism, abuse, or emotional escalation.

Special operations, nuclear transport, house raids, and mass deployment must follow strict planning, command approval, and accountability standards. Discord is the official communication backbone of command, and all major staff actions must remain transparent, documented, and professional.

Final Annotation: the strength of ERLC lies in disciplined leadership, not in personal influence or uncontrolled authority.
