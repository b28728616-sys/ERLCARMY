Convoy Standard Operating Procedure ▼

1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure governs all Department of Transportation convoys in Emergency Response: Liberty County. It establishes a clear chain of command, standardized vehicle positions, radio procedures, safety rules, and disciplinary expectations.

The convoy's goals are to:

  • Move DOT personnel and equipment safely.
  • Maintain a professional and organized formation.
  • Minimize disruption to civilian traffic and roleplay.
  • Coordinate effectively with police, fire, EMS, and other departments.
  • Provide consistent procedures for planned events and emergency deployments.

2. Chain of Command

Authority flows from the Convoy Commander through designated supervisors to individual drivers. Orders should follow this chain unless immediate action is needed to prevent a collision or protect a player.

Transfer of command

If the Convoy Commander disconnects, becomes unavailable, or leaves the convoy, authority transfers in this order:

  1. Deputy Commander
  2. Highest-ranking Convoy Supervisor
  3. Lead Driver
  4. Most senior Section Leader
  5. Rear Guard

The person assuming command must announce:

"All DOT units, be advised: [call sign] is assuming convoy command."

Only one person may serve as the active Convoy Commander.

4. Convoy Roles

Convoy Commander

The Convoy Commander has final operational authority. This position approves the route, destination, formation, and departure; coordinates with other department commanders; decides whether the convoy continues, reroutes, stops, or cancels; authorizes emergency exceptions; handles serious disciplinary or safety incidents; and conducts or delegates the pre-convoy briefing.

Deputy Commander

Tracks personnel, assignments, and convoy status; relays orders when the main radio channel is congested; assumes command when the Convoy Commander is unavailable; and helps investigate separation, collisions, or rule violations.

Convoy Supervisor

Watches spacing, speed, lane discipline, and vehicle behavior; corrects minor mistakes over the radio; organizes staging and parking; reports unsafe conduct to command; and may order an immediate safety stop.

Lead Driver

Knows the complete route before departure; sets a safe and consistent speed; announces turns, hazards, lane changes, and stops; and avoids sudden braking or last-second turns.

Section Leader

Convoys with more than eight DOT vehicles should be divided into sections of approximately four to six vehicles. Section Leaders maintain accountability for assigned units, relay instructions, report missing or damaged vehicles, and keep the section organized.

Rear Guard

Confirms that all vehicles clear intersections and turns; reports gaps, breakdowns, collisions, and trailing traffic; remains at the rear unless directed otherwise; and announces when the full convoy has arrived.

Operators

Every Operator must attend the briefing, use the assigned vehicle and call sign, maintain safe spacing and lane position, monitor the convoy radio channel, follow lawful orders, and report problems promptly and clearly.

Trainees

Trainees may participate only when assigned to a supervisor or experienced Operator. They may not serve as Lead Driver, Rear Guard, Section Leader, or Convoy Commander.

The route should favor wide roads, manageable intersections, and locations that can accommodate the entire convoy.

6. Staging Procedure

All vehicles report to the designated staging area. Operators park in their assigned order. Section Leaders inspect their groups. Supervisors check vehicle condition, appearance, and staffing. Command conducts the briefing. Each unit performs a radio check. DOT Lead confirms route readiness. DOT Rear confirms all vehicles are in formation. Command authorizes departure.

Required briefing

  • Convoy purpose and destination
  • Primary and alternate routes
  • Vehicle order
  • Authorized speed
  • Radio channel and call signs
  • Expected hazards
  • Separation and collision procedures
  • Rules for emergency lighting
  • Arrival and parking instructions

7. Vehicle Standards

Every convoy vehicle must be appropriate for the mission, use approved DOT livery, be repaired and fueled before staging, have a trained driver, and remain visually professional. Heavy vehicles should be near the front-middle of the formation. Recovery vehicles should normally remain near the rear.

8. Lighting and Warning Equipment

Staging

Use parking lights or hazard lights as directed. Avoid unnecessary siren use. Keep warning equipment limited so commands remain visible and clear.

Normal movement

DOT warning lights may be activated for visibility. Sirens should remain off unless specifically authorized. Headlights should be used at night or during poor visibility.

Roadside operations

Activate warning lights before slowing. Park to create a safe work zone. Use cones, signs, arrow boards, or blockers. Face equipment in the correct traffic direction. Emergency lights do not grant police-style right-of-way.

9. Speed and Spacing

Command should set a convoy speed appropriate for the slowest vehicle and current traffic conditions.

Road typeRecommended maximum
Parking lot or staging area10 mph
Dense city streets25 mph
Open city roads35 mph
County roads40 mph
Highway50 mph
Hazard or work zone10-20 mph

Following distance

  • Maintain approximately two vehicle lengths at low speeds.
  • Maintain approximately three to four vehicle lengths at normal road speeds.
  • Increase spacing behind heavy trucks or during poor visibility.
  • If a civilian enters the formation, remain calm and create space.

10. Traffic Rules

DOT convoy drivers must remain in the correct lane, stop at red lights and stop signs (unless controlled by law enforcement), signal turns and lane changes, yield to emergency vehicles, avoid unnecessary use of opposing lanes, never drive on sidewalks, never block intersections unless authorized, and never perform dangerous overtakes within the convoy.

11. Intersection Procedure

Without a police escort

Obey all traffic signals. Do not enter unless there is enough space to clear the intersection. If the convoy is split by a red light, the front group continues at reduced speed or stops safely. DOT Lead must not stop in the middle of the road solely to keep the convoy together.

With a police escort

Proceed only when the escort clearly controls the intersection. Reduce speed and watch for civilians. Do not assume every lane is secured. Police escorts should not be ordered around by DOT personnel.

12. Lane Changes and Turns

DOT Lead announces planned maneuvers early. Drivers must not all swerve into another lane simultaneously. If a unit misses a turn, do not reverse into traffic or make an illegal U-turn — continue to a safe location and notify the Section Leader or Rear Guard.

15. Separation Procedure

A convoy is separated when units lose visual contact or cannot follow the planned route. The separated unit must continue driving safely, avoid speeding or running signals, notify its Section Leader or Rear Guard, provide its location and status, pull into a safe area if instructed, and rejoin only through an approved route.

"DOT Rear, DOT 14. I am separated near the farms, vehicle operational, awaiting instructions."

16. Collision Procedure

After a collision: stop safely without causing a second collision, activate warning lights, announce the incident and location, move vehicles out of travel lanes when possible, request police/fire/EMS/recovery as appropriate, do not argue with other players, preserve roleplay, and wait for command before rejoining.

17. Breakdown and Recovery Procedure

A disabled unit should announce: "Priority traffic, DOT 7 disabled on the eastbound shoulder near the tunnel." The disabled Operator must move to the shoulder, activate hazard and warning lights, remain clear of moving traffic, avoid blocking the convoy, and wait for recovery instructions.

18. Emergency Vehicle Procedure

When police, fire, or EMS approaches with emergency equipment active: DOT Lead announces the approaching vehicle, every unit moves to the appropriate side, drivers stop or slow as needed, and no unit reenters traffic until the emergency vehicle has passed.

19. Hostile Players and Convoy Interference

DOT personnel must not escalate conflict. If another player blocks, rams, follows, or disrupts the convoy: maintain formation when safe, do not retaliate, do not chase or surround the player, record identifying information, notify law enforcement or server staff, and reroute or stop if the situation is unsafe. Only law enforcement should conduct traffic stops, detentions, pursuits, or arrests.

20. Work-Zone Deployment

Upon arrival, deploy in sequence: police/traffic-control units secure the location, DOT Lead identifies the work-zone entrance, blocker/arrow-board vehicles position first, cone/sign crews establish advance warning, maintenance vehicles enter the protected area, recovery vehicles take assigned positions, and DOT Rear confirms the route and buffer zone.

21. Parking and Arrival

DOT Lead must enter the destination slowly and park in the assigned location. Rear Guard announces: "DOT Command, DOT Rear. All convoy units have arrived and are accounted for." Command then announces: Hold formation, Begin deployment, Break formation, or Return to staging.

22. Conduct Rules

All participants must follow Roblox and server rules, treat other players respectfully, remain in character during active roleplay, use realistic vehicle handling, follow the chain of command, correct mistakes without arguments, protect civilian and emergency access, and keep department information professional.

Prohibited conduct: reckless driving, drifting, racing, ramming or vehicle boxing, rank impersonation, unauthorized siren use, ignoring commands, intentional disruption, abusing disciplinary powers, harassment or discriminatory behavior, leaving without reporting, using DOT authority as police, exploiting/glitching, or evading server moderation.

27. Quick Rules for Operators

  • Know your position before departure.
  • Monitor the radio and acknowledge direct orders.
  • Obey traffic laws unless police are actively controlling the road.
  • Maintain safe spacing without creating avoidable gaps.
  • Never overtake another convoy vehicle without permission.
  • Do not speed to catch up after separation.
  • Yield immediately to emergency vehicles.
  • Report collisions, breakdowns, and lost contact.
  • Do not retaliate against disruptive players.
  • Remain with the convoy until command releases you.

28. Final Command Checklist

Before departure, DOT Command must confirm: mission and destination announced, primary and alternate routes established, vehicle order assigned, Lead Driver and Rear Guard designated, radio checks completed, speed and spacing rules announced, emergency procedures reviewed, police coordination completed, all vehicles ready and correctly positioned, and departure authorization given.

The convoy begins only after DOT Lead, DOT Rear, and DOT Command independently confirm readiness.

Combat Medical Team:ERLC ARMY ROLEPLAY ▼

1. Mission & Purpose

The ERLC Army Roleplay exists to provide organized military roleplay during large-scale emergencies, deployments, security operations, disaster response, convoys, training exercises, and other authorized roleplay scenarios.

The ERLC Army Roleplay is responsible for:

  • Conducting organized military roleplay.
  • Supporting authorized emergency-service departments.
  • Providing security during authorized operations.
  • Conducting military deployments.
  • Supporting disaster and emergency response.
  • Providing organized convoy operations.
  • Providing field medical support.
  • Conducting casualty evacuation roleplay.
  • Maintaining professional communications.
  • Maintaining military discipline.
  • Protecting the reputation and integrity of the organization.

Army personnel are expected to operate as a professional military organization while remaining within the boundaries of the ERLC server's roleplay rules.

2. Core Principles

  • 2.1 Mission First — Members should focus on accomplishing the assigned roleplay objective.
  • 2.2 Safety — Members should avoid unnecessary risk to themselves, teammates, civilians, and other players.
  • 2.3 Professionalism — Members represent the ERLC Army Roleplay whenever they are on duty.
  • 2.4 Teamwork — Army personnel shall operate as a coordinated unit.
  • 2.5 Chain of Command — Orders should normally travel through the established chain of command.
  • 2.6 Realism — Members should maintain realistic military roleplay appropriate for ERLC.
  • 2.7 Discipline — Members are expected to follow authorized orders.
  • 2.8 Integrity — Members shall not falsify reports, qualifications, activity, incidents, or military actions.

3. Chain of Command

The ERLC Army Roleplay chain of command shall be:

Army Commander → Deputy Army Commander → Chief of Staff → Command Staff → Battalion Commanders → Company Commanders → Platoon Leaders → Squad Leaders → Senior Soldiers → Soldiers → Recruit/Training Personnel

Chain of Command Rule: Members should contact the lowest appropriate level of leadership before escalating an issue.

Members may bypass the chain of command when:

  1. There is an immediate emergency.
  2. The next person in command is unavailable.
  3. The situation involves misconduct by the next person in command.
  4. A higher-ranking commander specifically requests direct communication.

4. Rank Responsibilities

Army Commander

The Army Commander is the highest-ranking authority within the ERLC Army Roleplay organization. Responsibilities include: overall Army management, establishing Army policy, appointing command staff, approving major disciplinary actions, coordinating with other department leadership, managing major deployments, establishing Army-wide standards, and overseeing subordinate commands. The Army Commander should focus primarily on strategic command rather than micromanaging individual soldiers.

Deputy Army Commander

The Deputy Army Commander assists the Army Commander and assumes command when the Commander is unavailable. Responsibilities: Army-wide oversight, command coordination, training oversight, operational supervision, policy enforcement, reviewing major incidents, and supervising senior command.

Chief of Staff

The Chief of Staff assists the Commander and Deputy Commander with the administration and organization of the Army. Responsibilities: coordinating command staff, reviewing operations, managing internal organization, assisting with training standards, coordinating large deployments, reviewing reports, and ensuring Army SOP compliance.

Command Staff

Command Staff are responsible for senior operational leadership. Responsibilities: supervising Army personnel, managing large-scale operations, coordinating deployments, conducting evaluations, handling disciplinary matters within their authority, coordinating with other departments, and ensuring personnel follow SOP.

Battalion Commander

Battalion Commanders manage large groups of Army personnel. Responsibilities: managing subordinate companies, planning deployments, assigning personnel, coordinating with command, supervising company commanders, and maintaining operational readiness.

Company Commander

Company Commanders manage individual companies. Responsibilities: managing platoons, organizing personnel, conducting formations, assigning missions, maintaining discipline, and reporting to Battalion Command.

Platoon Leader

Platoon Leaders manage platoon-level operations. Responsibilities: supervising squads, assigning objectives, conducting briefings, maintaining communications, reporting mission status, and supporting company leadership.

Squad Leader

Squad Leaders are the primary small-unit leaders. Responsibilities: leading soldiers during operations, maintaining formation, assigning positions, reporting to the Platoon Leader, mentoring junior soldiers, and maintaining squad discipline.

Senior Soldiers

Senior Soldiers assist leadership and serve as experienced members of the unit. Responsibilities: supporting squad leadership, mentoring junior personnel, assisting with training, and maintaining professional standards.

Soldiers

Soldiers perform assigned Army duties. Responsibilities: following authorized orders, participating in training, maintaining formations, performing assigned missions, and following Army SOP.

Recruit/Training Personnel

Recruit personnel are learning Army procedures. They should: remain with qualified personnel, follow instructions, ask questions when uncertain, avoid taking command without authorization, and complete required training.

5. General Army Rules

  1. Follow the Chain of Command — Do not intentionally disregard legitimate orders from authorized leadership.
  2. Remain Professional — No trolling, harassment, excessive arguing, discrimination, or disruptive behavior while representing the ERLC Army Roleplay organization.
  3. No Random Deployment — Do not deploy to major operations without authorization, assignment, or dispatch.
  4. No Unnecessary Engagement — Army personnel should not intentionally create conflicts or interfere with civilian roleplay.
  5. Stay in Your Role — Army personnel should not impersonate police, fire, EMS, or other departments.
  6. No Unrealistic Roleplay — Members must maintain reasonable ERLC military roleplay.
  7. Respect Other Departments — Police, fire, EMS, dispatch, civilians, and other departments shall be treated professionally.
  8. No Equipment Abuse — Army vehicles and equipment shall not be used for personal purposes while on duty.
  9. No Formation Disruption — Members should remain in assigned formations during organized movements.
  10. Follow Server Rules — Server rules always take priority over ERLC Army Roleplay SOP.

6. Professional Conduct

ERLC Army Roleplay personnel shall:

  • Use appropriate language.
  • Maintain military professionalism.
  • Follow radio procedures.
  • Respect leadership.
  • Respect civilians.
  • Respect allied departments.
  • Avoid unnecessary arguments.
  • Avoid power-tripping.
  • Avoid favoritism.
  • Maintain proper discipline.

Prohibited Behavior includes: RDM/VDM, trolling, mic spamming, false dispatches, equipment abuse, vehicle abuse, impersonating command, falsifying reports, intentionally disrupting another department, intentionally disobeying legitimate orders, and using Army status to avoid normal server rules.

7. Uniform & Equipment Standards

Army personnel shall use department-approved: uniforms, vehicles, identification, communications equipment, medical equipment when assigned, and department-approved accessories.

Members should not use equipment that their rank, assignment, or certification does not authorize.

Uniform Requirements: On-duty personnel should maintain correct uniform, correct identification, appropriate accessories, and professional appearance. Personal accessories should not interfere with identification or roleplay.

8. Communications

Army communications should be: CLEAR → CONCISE → PROFESSIONAL → RELEVANT

Avoid unnecessary radio chatter.

Recommended Format: UNIT → LOCATION → STATUS → REQUEST

Example: "Army-21 to Command, arriving at the designated staging area. Requesting further instructions."

Priority Traffic should be reserved for: major incidents, injured personnel, convoy emergencies, missing personnel, unsafe conditions, and major operational changes.

9. Dispatch & Callouts

When assigned to an operation, personnel should:

  1. Acknowledge the assignment.
  2. Identify their unit.
  3. Move to the designated staging area.
  4. Receive the operational briefing.
  5. Confirm their assignment.
  6. Deploy when authorized.
  7. Maintain communications.
  8. Complete the mission.
  9. Return to staging or base.
  10. Report mission completion.

Example: Command: "Army units, deploy to the Highway 24 staging area." Army-21: "Army-21 copies. En route to staging." Upon arrival: "Army-21 on scene and awaiting assignment."

10. Deployment Procedures

For a standard deployment:

  1. Phase 1 — Activation: Army command receives or authorizes a deployment.
  2. Phase 2 — Mobilization: Personnel gather at the designated assembly point.
  3. Phase 3 — Briefing: Command provides mission objective, location, expected conditions, known hazards, staging location, unit assignments, convoy information, medical arrangements, and communications plan.
  4. Phase 4 — Formation: Personnel organize into their assigned formations.
  5. Phase 5 — Movement: Units move toward the operational area.
  6. Phase 6 — Staging: Army personnel establish at the designated staging location.
  7. Phase 7 — Mission: Units perform their assigned objective.
  8. Phase 8 — Accountability: Leadership confirms personnel are accounted for.
  9. Phase 9 — Withdrawal: Command orders units to return or relocate.
  10. Phase 10 — Debrief: Leadership conducts a short after-action review.

11. Combat Zone Medical Procedures

These procedures are designed strictly for ERLC military roleplay.

Army medical support should divide high-risk areas into three general zones:

  • RED — UNSAFE / ACTIVE DANGER: Personnel should generally remain outside the immediate danger area.
  • YELLOW — CONTROLLED AREA: Medical personnel may stage and prepare.
  • GREEN — MEDICAL AREA: Patients are assessed, treated through roleplay, and prepared for transport.

Tactical Medical Rule: Army personnel should not intentionally place themselves into unnecessary danger. If a scene is unsafe, Army medical personnel should coordinate with scene command before entering.

12. Casualty Triage

For ERLC roleplay, Army medical personnel may use:

  • RED — CRITICAL: Patient requires immediate attention. Examples: severe roleplay injuries, unconscious patient, critical condition.
  • YELLOW — SERIOUS: Patient requires medical attention but can wait briefly.
  • GREEN — MINOR: Patient has minor injuries and can wait.
  • BLACK — DECEASED/UNRECOVERABLE IN ROLEPLAY: Used only when the roleplay circumstances establish that the patient is beyond medical assistance. Do not use this category casually.

13. Patient Stabilization Roleplay

Army medical personnel should use a consistent roleplay sequence:

  1. Step 1 — Scene Safety: Confirm the area is reasonably safe.
  2. Step 2 — Patient Contact: Identify the patient and establish communication.
  3. Step 3 — Initial Assessment: Roleplay checking responsiveness, breathing, major visible injuries, and general condition.
  4. Step 4 — Stabilization Roleplay: Perform appropriate fictional ERLC medical roleplay.
  5. Step 5 — Reassessment: Determine whether the patient is improving, stable, or worsening.
  6. Step 6 — Evacuation Decision: Determine whether the patient needs evacuation.
  7. Step 7 — Hospital Handoff: Provide the receiving medical team with a brief report.

14. Patient Transport

Before transporting:

  • Confirm the patient is ready.
  • Confirm an appropriate vehicle is available.
  • Notify command when necessary.
  • Select the receiving medical facility.
  • Maintain professional driving.

Transport Priority: Critical patients receive priority. Multiple patients should be distributed among available medical units when possible.

15. Hospital Handoff

Upon arrival, the transporting unit should provide a concise handoff:

  • Patient
  • Incident
  • Condition
  • Treatment Roleplay
  • Current Status
  • Additional Information

Example: "Soldier involved in a major vehicle incident. Patient was initially unconscious, received stabilization roleplay, and is now responsive but serious."

16. Downed Soldier / Responder Procedures

When an Army member or allied responder requires medical assistance:

  1. Notify command.
  2. Confirm the area is reasonably safe.
  3. Assign medical personnel.
  4. Move the patient to the medical area when appropriate.
  5. Perform medical roleplay.
  6. Determine evacuation requirements.
  7. Transport when necessary.
  8. Report the patient's status to command.

Army personnel should not abandon other patients simply because a soldier is injured unless prioritization is necessary.

17. Mass Casualty Incidents

A mass casualty incident is an event where available medical resources may become overwhelmed. Examples include: large vehicle crash, major disaster, large-scale ERLC incident, multiple injured soldiers, and large public events.

MCI Procedure:

  1. Declare MCI: "Army Medical Command declaring mass casualty incident."
  2. Establish Medical Command: A designated medical leader takes control of the medical response.
  3. Establish Treatment Area: Create a dedicated medical area.
  4. Begin Triage: Categorize patients.
  5. Request Additional Resources: Additional medical units may be requested.
  6. Begin Treatment: Prioritize patients according to severity.
  7. Evacuate: Coordinate transportation.
  8. Demobilize: Return personnel to normal duties once released.

18. Convoy Operations

Army convoys may be used for:

  • Military deployments.
  • Personnel movements.
  • Equipment movements.
  • Emergency response.
  • Medical evacuations.
  • Authorized protected movements.
  • Large-scale events.
  • Interdepartmental operations.

Every convoy should have one designated CONVOY COMMANDER. The Convoy Commander controls convoy movement unless command authority is transferred.

Position 1 — Lead / Scout

The Lead Unit travels at the front. Responsibilities include: following the designated route, identifying roleplay hazards, maintaining reasonable speed, notifying convoy command of problems, and avoiding unnecessary stops. Position 2 — Convoy Commander

The Convoy Commander controls the movement. Responsibilities include: setting convoy speed, setting route, ordering stops, coordinating with other departments, maintaining communications, handling convoy emergencies, and deciding when the convoy continues or stops.

Position 3 — Main Element

The main Army vehicles travel in the central portion of the convoy. This may include: personnel carriers, transport vehicles, command vehicles, and standard Army vehicles. The main element should remain together.

Position 4 — Support / Medical Element

Support and medical vehicles follow the main element. They may include: ambulances, medical support vehicles, recovery/support vehicles, and additional personnel. Their purpose is to provide support if a convoy member requires assistance.

Position 5 — Rear / Trailing Unit

The Rear Unit remains at the back. Responsibilities include: ensuring no convoy unit is left behind, reporting separation, assisting disabled vehicles, and notifying the Convoy Commander of problems.

20. Convoy Rules

  1. Do not pass another convoy vehicle without authorization.
  2. Maintain reasonable spacing.
  3. Do not intentionally break formation.
  4. Follow the Convoy Commander.
  5. Do not use emergency equipment unnecessarily.
  6. If separated, notify convoy command.
  7. If your vehicle becomes disabled, notify the convoy.
  8. Do not independently pursue suspects unless specifically authorized by the appropriate command structure.
  9. Maintain your assigned convoy position.
  10. The Convoy Commander may temporarily change the formation when necessary.

21. Security & Medical Coordination

The ERLC Army Roleplay organization may work alongside law enforcement, fire, EMS, and other departments during major incidents.

The general division of responsibilities is:

  • Law Enforcement: Law enforcement operations, scene security, suspect apprehension, civilian law enforcement.
  • ERLC Army Roleplay: Authorized military operations, military security, personnel movements, deployment operations, military logistics, assigned support missions.
  • EMS / Medical Personnel: Medical treatment, patient care, medical staging, patient transportation.
  • Fire/Rescue: Fire suppression, rescue, extrication roleplay, specialized rescue.
  • Dispatch: Communications, resource coordination, unit tracking, call assignment.

22. Scene Command

Every major operation should have a clearly identified command structure:

Incident Commander → Army Command → Battalion/Company Command → Platoon Leader → Squad Leader → Soldiers

Army personnel should report through their designated military chain of command rather than independently taking control of an entire scene.

23. Mutual Aid

When another department requests Army assistance:

  1. Confirm the request.
  2. Notify Army command.
  3. Assign available personnel.
  4. Establish a staging area.
  5. Report to the requesting department's command.
  6. Perform the assigned mission.
  7. Maintain Army chain of command.
  8. Clear when released.

27. Emergency Authority

During an emergency, the highest-ranking available Army member may temporarily assume operational command when no designated commander is present.

Examples: Senior Soldier → Temporary Squad Lead, or Squad Leader → Temporary Platoon Lead

Once the appropriate commander arrives, command should be transferred when appropriate. Temporary emergency authority should only be used for the immediate situation and should not be abused to make permanent organizational decisions.

28. Failure to Follow SOP

Failure to follow this manual may result in disciplinary action. Leadership should consider: severity of the violation, intent, previous history, impact on roleplay, whether the member corrected the issue, whether the situation was an emergency, and exceptions.

Exceptions: SOP may be temporarily deviated from when necessary to protect personnel, protect civilians, prevent an emergency from escalating, follow a higher-level server rule, or respond to unexpected circumstances. Any significant deviation should be reported to leadership afterward.

Tier 3

ODA (Green Berets)

ODA teams specialize in unconventional warfare, partner-force support, training, advising, and complex missions requiring judgment and discretion.

Rank Structure

  • Officer leadership: Captain (CPT) ODA commander; First Lieutenant (1LT) assistant ODA commander
  • Warrant expertise: Chief Warrant Officer 3-2 (CW3-CW2) technical and operational specialists
  • Senior enlisted: Sergeant First Class (SFC) team sergeant; Master Sergeant (MSG) senior advisor
  • Team personnel: Staff Sergeant (SSG), Sergeant (SGT), Specialist (SPC), and junior enlisted support

Tier 2

Rangers

Rangers provide highly trained light-infantry capability for direct action, raids, reconnaissance, security, and rapid-response missions.

Rank Structure

  • Battalion leadership: Lieutenant Colonel (LTC) battalion commander; Command Sergeant Major (CSM) senior enlisted advisor
  • Company leadership: Captain (CPT) company commander; First Sergeant (1SG) company senior enlisted leader
  • Platoon leadership: First Lieutenant (1LT) or Second Lieutenant (2LT) platoon leader; Sergeant First Class (SFC) platoon sergeant
  • Squad leadership: Staff Sergeant (SSG), Sergeant (SGT), Specialist (SPC), and junior enlisted personnel

Tier 1

1st Special Forces Operational Detachment-Delta

Delta Force handles the most sensitive and demanding missions, including high-risk counterterrorism, hostage rescue, special reconnaissance, and precision action.

Rank Structure

  • Unit leadership: Colonel (COL) group commander; Lieutenant Colonel (LTC) squadron commander
  • Operational leadership: Major (MAJ) or Captain (CPT) troop commander; Command Sergeant Major (CSM) senior enlisted advisor
  • Technical leadership: Chief Warrant Officer 5-1 (CW5-CW1) specialists assigned to aviation, communications, intelligence, or logistics
  • Operator leadership: Sergeant Major (SGM), First Sergeant (1SG), Master Sergeant (MSG), and Sergeant First Class (SFC)
  • Operators and support: Staff Sergeant (SSG), Sergeant (SGT), Specialist (SPC), Private First Class (PFC), and Private (PV2/PVT)

Rules of Engagement

Special Forces ROE

All tiers follow the same rules of engagement as every other soldier operating in a hostile area. Complex missions do not remove the requirements for lawful orders, positive identification, necessity, proportionality, and command accountability.

  • Use force only when authorized, necessary, and proportionate to the threat.
  • Distinguish confirmed hostile action from suspicion or an unverified assumption.
  • When time permits, pause, communicate, de-escalate, and seek command guidance.
  • Document the facts, warnings, evidence, and proportionality of serious use-of-force decisions.
  • Expect independent review when an action is disputed; good intent does not replace a defensible justification.
  • No tier may conduct extrajudicial action, retaliatory force, or unauthorized targeting.
